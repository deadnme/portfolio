The help desk could reset a Domain Admin’s password

I gave the help desk the right to reset passwords and nothing else. Testing what it couldn’t do showed it could take over the domain.

Area
Active Directory
Project
Windows AD homelab

What I saw

While testing a password-reset ticket as Maya, a help desk user, I also checked that she couldn’t reset the password of adm.ethan.brooks, an IT admin account that is a Domain Admin. She could. With that, Maya could have signed in as a Domain Admin and taken over the whole domain.

Why it happened

I had created the adm.* admin accounts inside the IT department’s OU, and the help desk’s “reset password” delegation covers every account under that part of the tree. Windows does protect Domain Admin accounts with a background process, AdminSDHolder (SDProp), that strips inherited permissions, but it only runs every 60 minutes, and the account was newer than that.

How I found it

Testing the negative case on purpose: after each delegation I tried things the help desk should not be able to do, signed in as a help desk user.

The fix

A dedicated Admin Accounts OU outside any help desk delegation, and the admin accounts moved into it. Then I re-tested as Maya:

PS> Set-ADAccountPassword adm.ethan.brooks -Reset ... -Credential $maya
Denied: Access is denied

My user-creation script now puts admin accounts in that OU from the start.

Lesson: delegation flows down to everything in the OU. Keep privileged accounts in their own OU, and test what the help desk can’t do as well as what it can.
